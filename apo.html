The Hidden Threat in One-Step ELISA

ELISA is a well-established technique used worldwide to quantify analytes in diverse biological samples. Clinicians rely on its accuracy and precision to administer patient care. However, these results must be held with great scrutiny, as the assay is subject to errors caused by interfering substances in the sample matrix.

Among the most insidious of these interferences are human autoantibodies against the Horseradish Peroxidase (HRP) label — a critical component of countless immunoassay systems.

The "One-Step" Vulnerability

Anti-HRP autoantibodies only interfere in one-step (competitive) EIA formats.

  • Two-step assays: Autoantibodies washed away before HRP conjugate added → No interference
  • One-step assays (e.g., competitive T3/T4 ELISA): Sample and HRP-conjugate incubated together → Autoantibodies bind HRP and block activity

Clinical consequence: False elevation of T3/T4 → unnecessary treatment or surgery.

Schematic of competitive one-step EIA: Anti-HRP antibodies bind HRP-T3 conjugate during simultaneous incubation, blocking signal → false high T3.

The Biological Link: Thyroid Autoimmunity

Why do some patients develop anti-HRP antibodies? Molecular mimicry between human Thyroid Peroxidase (TPO) and Horseradish Peroxidase (HRP).

The clinical reality: Approximately 8% of males and 10-15% of females without known thyroid disease still harbor detectable thyroid autoantibodies. Any patient with autoimmune thyroid disease (or even subclinical autoimmunity) is at risk for cross-reactive anti-HRP antibodies that can ruin your one-step T3/T4 ELISA results.

Published Evidence

A landmark case report proving anti-HRP autoantibodies cause spurious results in one-step competitive immunoassays.

Click to open the full abstract on PubMed (PMID: 29175172)

Key findings from Lim et al., Clin Chim Acta. 2018

  • Two sisters with mild hirsutism → grossly elevated androstenedione (>34.9 nmol/L) by one-step HRP-based immunoassay
  • LC-MS/MS (reference method) → normal levels
  • Neutralization with HRP-conjugate → normalized results (9.5 and 12.5 nmol/L)
  • Author conclusion: "The interference is due to autoantibodies against the HRP label used in the immunoassay. This is the first report of autoantibodies against the HRP label."

❌ Expensive, not scalable, unavailable in most routine labs

How it works (no pre-incubation)

Poly Apo HRP is supplied in highly concentrated form and added by the kit manufacturer directly to the reaction buffer that contains the HRP conjugate.
